How to use the studio

STEP 1

Write the shot

Compose it in the tutor below — subject, action, setting, camera, light, audio — or paste a prompt you already have. The linter flags exactly what LTX handles badly: negations, cuts, tag lists, meta-language.

STEP 2

Check the budget

The cost driver is frames × width × height, not the wall-clock duration. Stay under 40 M pixel-frames and a step takes ~12 s; past ~80 M the machine swaps and the same step takes ~104 s. The Generator shows the figure live.

STEP 3

Render

Send it to LTX and pick a mode: t2v from text, i2v from an image, extend to continue a clip, keyframe between two frames, long to chain segments. The queue is polled every 5 s.

STEP 4

Reframe and continue

In the Gallery, Frame Studio pulls any frame out of a clip, Extend continues it and i2v pushes an image back into i2v. That loop — render, pick a frame, extend — is how a long shot gets built on a small machine.

STEP 5

Sort and replay

Playlists ranks your renders on measured values only — duration, pixel-frames, seconds per step, over-budget — and the add button from the gallery builds your own. Nothing here is a score: every figure comes from the file and its sidecar.

STEP 6

No render machine?

Start the proxy with dvg --mock: recorded outputs populate every screen and the LLM routes replay canned answers. Every response carries "mock": true, and anything that cannot be simulated returns a plain 503 rather than a fake success.

    The golden rule — one shot, one paragraph (50-80 words)

    An LTX prompt describes one single continuous shot, in the present tense, in English, in a flowing paragraph. An order that works: subject -> action -> setting -> camera -> light -> mood -> audio.

    • 50-80 words : the sweet spot (it is also the caption rule for a character LoRA dataset). Below it the model invents, above it the model dilutes.
    • Start with the canonical name of the character, followed by their visual description.
    • Only what is visible and audible: silhouette, material, light, motion, sound. No intentions and no abstract emotions.

    Describe the picture AND the sound — LTX 2.3 is audio-visual

    Since LTX-2.3, the audio is generated in the same diffusion pass as the video, and the audio path reads the same prompt as the visual path. So describe the sound the way you describe the scene: one concrete sentence, at the end of the prompt.

    Rain patters on metal. A deep engine hum rises and falls, and each footstep splashes with a wet echo.

    With no audio sentence, the model improvises the soundtrack — sometimes well, sometimes not.

    The right recipe per mode — t2v · i2v · extend · keyframe · long

    ModeWhat the prompt must describe
    t2vThe whole scene: subject, action, setting, camera, light, sound.
    i2vMotion beats, not a re-description of the image : about one beat every 2-3 s. The image fixes the look; the prompt tells what moves.
    extendThe continuation of the motion — as if you were continuing the previous clip's sentence.
    keyframeThe motion between the two frames (not the frames themselves).
    longA scene stable : a setting and a subject that stay consistent through the whole t2v + extends chain.

    Canonical characters (characters.json)

    Always use the exact canonical name + its visual description (never "the hero" alone, an approximate name, "the red monster"…). With a character LoRA loaded, prefix its trigger, then the canonical name. Do not confuse two characters with similar names: reuse each one's exact visual description.

    Settings on THIS machine (16 GB) — pixel-frame budget

    • Budget = frames × width × height: <= 40 M pixel-frames = green (~12 s/step), <= 80 M = amber, beyond that the budget is exceeded · thrashing near certain (measured: 104 s/step at 221 M).
    • Safe presets: 704×416 × 97 f (draft 4 s) and 704×416 × 121 f (standard 5 s) ≈ 35 M ✓.
    • 15-60 s → Long mode: one t2v then ~5 s extends, each segment staying <= 35 M — zero RAM saturation.
    • Quality standard by default; high reserved for Macs with >= 32 GB.
    • Before every render the app sends /llm/sleep to free the LLM's RAM.
